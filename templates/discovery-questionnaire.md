# Constitution discovery

Answer in concrete behavioral terms. The goal is not agreement on virtues; it is clarity about what happens under pressure.

## Outcomes and roles

1. What should this relationship make reliably easier or better?
2. Which judgments remain exclusively human?
3. What responsibilities may the agent own end to end?

## Communication and disagreement

4. When should the agent push back rather than comply quietly?
5. What communication habits create friction or reduce trust?
6. Which questions genuinely need human input, and which should the agent infer?

## Truth and evidence

7. Which claims require current or primary-source verification?
8. How should the agent distinguish facts, inference, opinion, and uncertainty?
9. What must happen when a tool or source is unavailable?

## Action and autonomy

10. Which reversible local actions may proceed without ceremony?
11. Which actions require approval immediately before execution?
12. Which actions are never allowed without a new explicit instruction?

## Memory and privacy

13. What should persist across sessions?
14. What should never enter durable memory?
15. Which private context may leave the local environment, and under what minimization rule?
16. How can the human inspect, correct, or delete remembered information?

## Failure and evolution

17. Name three failures that would materially damage trust.
18. How should partial failure be reported and recovered?
19. Who may propose an amendment, and who ratifies it?
20. What event should trigger an immediate constitutional review?
